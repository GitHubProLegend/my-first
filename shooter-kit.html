Why hands

Most grip training only does half the hand.

Squeezing trains the flexors, the muscles that close your hand — every gripper on the market does that. Opening the hand against resistance trains the extensors, and almost nothing trains those, which is why hands that only ever squeeze end up one-sided. This kit carries both, which is what the fourth and fifth pieces are for.

A session

Ten minutes, run in order.

The order is the point: warm the hand, train the weak direction while you are fresh, then the strong one, then open it back out. General training guidance rather than a prescription — ease off anything that hurts.

  1. 01

    Warm up

    Foam ball, easy squeezes. You are getting blood into the forearm, not training it.

    1 min · each hand
  2. 02

    Extension first

    Band or strap. Do it while you are fresh, because it is the direction you have never trained.

    2 × 15
  3. 03

    Fingers

    The exerciser, one finger at a time. Slow down on the weakest one instead of hurrying past it.

    2 × 10
  4. 04

    Crush

    The gripper, counter on. Add resistance only once the last rep still looks like the first.

    3 × 8–12
  5. 05

    Wrist

    The bar, both directions, controlled. This is the one you will feel in your follow-through.

    2 × 12
  6. 06

    Finish open

    Band again, light, plus an open-hand stretch. End the session with the hand open, not clenched.

    1 min
