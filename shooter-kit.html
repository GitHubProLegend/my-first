The geometry

The hoop the ball actually sees.

A rim is 18″ across, but only to a ball falling straight down. Come in shallow and the opening the ball meets is an ellipse, and it narrows fast. This is the whole argument for arc, and it is arithmetic rather than opinion.

Effective opening = 18″ × sin(entry angle). Margin is what is left once the ball is in it.

Seven degrees of arc is the difference between three and a bit inches of room and less than one. That is why a flat shooter lives and dies on perfect alignment, and why the same miss that rattles out for them drops for someone shooting at 45°.

The five-spot ladder

One session, run in order.

The markers set these five spots. The order is the point: corners are the shortest three-pointers on the floor, so you start where the shot is easiest and work toward the one that needs the most arc.

  1. 01

    Right corner

    Flattest release of the five. Feet square to the baseline, and no drift toward the line.

    22′0″ · 10 makes
  2. 02

    Right wing

    The extra 21 inches show up as arc, not effort. Same release, higher finish.

    23′9″ · 10 makes
  3. 03

    Top of the key

    Squarest look at the rim, so it is the honest read on your arc. Check the card here.

    23′9″ · 10 makes
  4. 04

    Left wing

    Mirror of 02. Any difference between the two sides is a guide-hand problem.

    23′9″ · 10 makes
  5. 05

    Left corner

    Finish where you started, tired. If the arc holds here, it will hold in a game.

    22′0″ · 10 makes
